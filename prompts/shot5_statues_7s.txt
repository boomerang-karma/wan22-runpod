Indoors in a bright hall with a white marble floor, two young girls stand perfectly still in front of a bronze sculpture of three seated children, copying the statues: the girl in the middle, in a blue top and dark tulle skirt, keeps both hands pressed over her eyes the whole time, and the girl on the right, in a cream party dress with a sparkly headband, keeps both hands pressed over her mouth the whole time. The girls never lower their hands; they only giggle silently behind them, shoulders shaking a little. Behind them the three bronze statues are the classic hear no evil, see no evil, speak no evil trio, each with a different gesture: the statue on the left presses its palms flat against the sides of its head over its ears, elbows pointing outward, eyes open; the statue in the middle covers its eyes with both hands; the statue on the right covers its mouth with both hands, eyes open. For the first moment all three hold these poses. Then the statues magically come alive and slowly lower their hands to their laps at the same time, look around, and glance curiously at the two girls. Then, one after another, they return to their own different gestures: the left statue lifts its hands to the sides of its head and covers its ears, the middle statue lifts its hands and covers its eyes, and the right statue lifts its hands and covers its mouth, all three still shiny bronze, ending clearly as hear no evil, see no evil, speak no evil. Camera locked off and steady, no zoom, natural indoor lighting, warm, magical, playful family moment. Sound: soft echo of a large indoor hall, faint metallic creaks as each bronze statue moves, muffled giggles from the girls behind their hands.
