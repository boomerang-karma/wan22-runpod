Indoors in a bright hall with a white marble floor, a bronze sculpture of three seated children stands on a white pedestal: the statue on the left presses its palms over its ears, the middle statue covers its eyes, the statue on the right covers its mouth. In front of it, two young girls stand perfectly still copying the statues: the girl in the middle, in a blue top and dark tulle skirt, keeps both hands over her eyes the whole time, and the girl on the right, in a cream party dress with a sparkly headband, keeps both hands over her mouth the whole time; they only giggle silently behind their hands. On the floor at the front left sits a baby girl in a light blue t-shirt with a white flower and white leggings, bangles on her wrists. The bronze statues magically come alive: they slowly lower their hands to their laps and look down curiously at the baby. The baby looks up at them, then playfully mocks them, copying their poses one after another: she pats both little hands over her ears, then covers her eyes, then covers her mouth, and bounces happily with a big toothless grin. The statues then lift their hands back to their ears, eyes and mouth, ending in their original hear no evil, see no evil, speak no evil poses, while the baby claps her hands.
